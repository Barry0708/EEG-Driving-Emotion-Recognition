# EEG-Driving-Emotion-Recognition
In the field of traffic safety, a driver's emotional state significantly impacts driving safety. Negative emotions such as anger and anxiety can reduce a driver's reaction speed and judgment, increasing the risk of accidents. Existing driver assistance systems primarily focus on detecting the external environment and lack attention to the driver's internal state. Therefore, we aim to provide more comprehensive safety assistance by detecting driving emotions in real time.
This project aims to develop a real-time driver emotion recognition system based on electroencephalogram (EEG) data and explore the relationship between driver emotional changes and driving behavior. By calculating the power of different EEG bands, we establish an emotion recognition model. Using the CARLA driving simulator to simulate real-world traffic situations, we validate the system's recognition performance and analyze the impact of emotions on driving behavior.
The research consists of two experimental phases. The first phase aims to select dashcam videos that evoke both negative and calm emotions. These videos primarily focus on traffic events that trigger emotional shifts, such as sudden car accidents. After analyzing the emotional scores assigned by the subjects to each video, we select videos that evoke negative emotions for the next phase of the experiment. The second phase aimed to develop an emotion recognition system based on brainwaves. During the experiment, participants wore a 32-channel brainwave cap to collect brainwave signals while watching emotional videos. Next, a machine learning model was used to train a system capable of instantly identifying driving emotions. This system was then integrated with the CARLA simulator to simulate negative road events to verify its recognition performance and changes in driving behavior.
